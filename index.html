Abstract

World-Action Models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm of robot manipulation policies that jointly generate future video and actions. Successful manipulation requires both identifying where to interact and modeling how the robot and objects move. Yet standard adaptation methods, such as full fine-tuning and LoRA, do not explicitly select parameters aligned with these manipulation-specific capabilities. Motivated by this challenge, we discover two functionally specialized families of attention heads: Next-Interaction Heads, which localize upcoming grasp-and-release targets, and Motion Heads, which track movement across frames. Furthermore, we introduce HeadLight, a parameter-efficient refinement method that jointly adapts Next-Interaction Heads and Motion Heads.

On RoboTwin tasks unseen during fine-tuning, HeadLight achieves 62.28% success, compared with 57.89% for full fine-tuning and 58.66% for LoRA, while updating only 1.1% of parameters. Inference-time ablations further validate the functional importance of these heads: ablating Motion Heads reduces manipulation success from 57.89% to 0.94%, while ablating Next-Interaction Heads lowers it to 40.61%, both substantially more than ablating equally many random heads. Finally, we show that our approach is effective across multiple WAM architectures and real-robot manipulation settings.

Two families of specialized heads

Manipulation alternates between discrete contact events (grasp, release) and continuous motion. We score every one of the 720 video attention heads in a frozen WAM on both axes. A small, disjoint set of heads stands out on each.

24 heads

Next-Interaction Heads

Where to interact next

To find them, we mask the token covering the upcoming interaction target and measure how much each head's output changes. We compare that change against four control tokens: a random token, one equidistant from the gripper, the previous target, and the target after next. A head counts only if the true next target matters more than every control.

$$S_N(\ell,h)=\min_{c}\,\log\frac{\mathbb{E}_f\!\left[\Delta_{\ell,h}(\mathcal{T})\right]}{\mathbb{E}_f\!\left[\Delta_{\ell,h}(\mathcal{C}_c)\right]}$$

The selected head locks onto the grasp target and ignores the distractor. A same-layer unselected head attends diffusely.

48 heads

Motion Heads

How the robot and objects move

Each head's cross-frame attention defines a motion field: every patch points to where it attends in the previous frame. We score heads by how well that field agrees with optical flow, using cosine similarity.

$$S_M(\ell,h)=\mathbb{E}_{f,p}\!\left[\cos\!\big(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{f}_{\ell,h}(p),\,\mathbf{u}^{f}(p)\big)\right]$$

Motion Head L7h0 tracks the moving arm and gripper. Random head L7h20 gives disorganized offsets.

Method

HeadLight is a one-time head discovery step, a causal check, and a targeted fine-tune. Both pathways use a single forward pass. Updates are additive, so they merge into the base weights and inference cost stays the same.

  1. 1

    Find the two head groups

    We run one teacher-forced pass of the frozen WAM over a few demonstrations per task and score all 720 video heads. The top KN=24 by SN and the top KM=48 by SM are kept. No gradients are needed, and the sets are reused across downstream tasks.

  2. 2

    Verify they matter

    Removing each group at inference causes a distinct failure. Without Next-Interaction Heads, the robot misses the cup. Without Motion Heads, the predicted video shows ghosted, inflated objects and execution stalls. Removing the same number of random heads leaves the rollout largely intact.

  3. 3

    Fine-tune only those heads

    Video pathway: the video loss updates only the 24 Next-Interaction Heads, which sharpens where grasps and releases appear in the predicted video. Action pathway: the video Motion Heads stay frozen. The action loss updates their 48 paired action heads, so actions follow the predicted motion.

Generalization to unseen tasks

Every method is adapted on 43 seen RoboTwin 2.0 tasks and evaluated on 6 unseen tasks (100 trials × 3 seeds each). LoRA's rank is chosen to match HeadLight's parameter budget (113.5M vs. 113.3M).

Real-robot manipulation

This uses a Franka Panda with a third-person ZED 2i and a wrist-mounted ZED Mini. Policies are adapted on four seen tasks (100 teleoperated demos each). They are tested on those four tasks and on four unseen tasks that recombine the same objects into new goals. Each task gets 20 rollouts.
